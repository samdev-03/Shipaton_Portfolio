# Design rationale and review guide

The visual system uses an expressive serif for the central promise and a clear sans serif for work. DM Serif Display and DM Sans are bundled, avoiding runtime font-service requests. Four related palettes identify distinct products: leaf green for rehearsal, blue for shared care, warm apricot for meals and lavender for quotes. Original SVG brand marks also produce the 1024px app icons.

Rehearsal Room places the next action before a scenario catalog. A turn identifies speaker, text and retry opportunity. Feedback uses a short supportive observation and an actionable next attempt. Ratings are explicitly informal wording cues. The self-reflection is labelled as the user's feeling, not an assessment. The retry branch is the interaction to demonstrate to design judges.

Care Relay distinguishes intention, acknowledgment and completion with words as well as color. The task form collapses once a task exists, leaving shared responsibility visible. Handoff never marks a task complete implicitly. Destructive circle deletion explains the effect on every member before confirmation.

Meal Patch starts from constraints people can actually control. No judgmental food labels, body metrics or default restrictive goals appear. Every addition lists ingredients and steps. Price estimates are per addition. Quote to Cash narrows the audience to independent cleaners, uses plain scope language and keeps totals visible before save. Public approval explains exactly what accepting does.

Buttons have generous touch targets, text inputs have accessible labels, errors use live announcements, selections expose accessibility state, and layouts scroll at narrow widths. The app does not depend on color alone. There is no mandatory motion effect or audio; speech playback is an explicit action. A large responsive layout reuses the same workflow without replacing it with a different product.

Before publication, test VoiceOver and TalkBack reading order, large text sizes, keyboard visibility on the lowest supported devices, touch targets with accessibility settings, contrast for all disabled/selected states, switch announcements, permission denial and interrupted network behavior. Browser screenshots and semantic labels are useful evidence but not a completed assistive-technology audit.

Do not claim advanced animations, independent accessibility certification, influencer endorsement, validated psychological measurement or clinical nutrition expertise. The strongest design story is restraint plus a working, understandable next step.
